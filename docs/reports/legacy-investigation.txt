## What the code says

**World and map (#1, #3)**
- The generator works at planet scale, but everything that varies the land (hills, climate, biome) changes over 60–2,200 km. Your zone is 16 km across, so it gets one landscape: the same gentle hills, one biome, and about 300 trees per hectare plus bushes and ferns.
- There is no code for rivers, lakes, cliffs or plateaus at all.
- hollow-orbit felt richer because its planet had a 3.6 km radius, so features were about 1,800× closer together. It also had explicit valleys, lakes, mesas and canyons.
- Assets:
  - Only 8 ground textures reach the game, 6 of them from your 34 Megascans sets.
  - None of the 62 scanned cliffs, rocks and pieces of deadwood are used.
  - Using the 4K files at full size wouldn't look sharper at our tiling. What's missing is variety and the scanned meshes.
- The map covers a 3.9 km square, about 6% of the zone, and opens zoomed in to 1.5 km. A flat map is fine at zone scale; a planet view would be a separate screen.

**Performance (#23)**
- Every axe hit makes the client rebuild and re-upload all the plants around you on the main thread. That's a 20–30 ms frame per swing, even though a hit that doesn't fell the tree changes nothing you can see. The same rebuild runs every 8 m you walk.
- The moon's light draws the forest's shadows a second time in daytime whenever the moon is up. That's likely part of your 30–80 fps range.
- The hit's chips and sound wait for the server. I estimate 70–150 ms after the visible contact, even when you host.
- The arms swing every 0.855 s, the server every 0.867 s, so the two drift apart. That's the desync you saw.

**Ships (#28, #29)**
- All six flight axes exist.
- Q is roll, but it also opens crafting, because the interface's keys don't know you're seated. Any open screen then zeroes every flight axis, so one press of Q and you can't fly.
- Space is the ship's "up". Only F leaves the seat, and only when the ship is at rest. Nothing on screen lists the keys.
- Noclip speed is fixed at 10 m/s, or 40 m/s with Shift.

**Weapons**

Three of these were shortcuts earlier sessions chose without asking you: recoil that returns by itself, sights that don't use their eye point, and attachments added by dropping and removed with a button. You're now rejecting exactly those.

## #30 and the architecture

You're right to be suspicious. What was approved yesterday holds for what it covers: every attack goes through one damage entry point, every E through one interaction system, every placement through one pipeline. The leaks are at the edges:

- **Damage:** harm that isn't an attack (falls, hunger, crashes) skips that entry point and calls two lower-level functions that apply damage and kill directly. God mode is checked in the first of them. A wrecked ship (any impact above about 23 m/s) calls the second directly, and four other places do the same.
- **Keys:** they don't know whether you're on foot, seated or in a menu. Three separate handlers hard-code keys, and the pilot's keys exist in two copies.
- **Main-thread work:** nothing limits it per frame (the plants, distant tree billboards, the sky table).
- **Art versus rules:** model sizes and gameplay sizes are never compared, and the art import never checks a mesh for duplicate faces.
- **Unused data:** some data is never read (the weapon light, sights' eye points, casing sounds). The tests check that the data exists, not that anything uses it.
- **One shared model for every world object (P6):** still not done, and it's the biggest piece. Each kind of object still has its own storage, network sync and save code.

Each package below fixes its bugs and adds a guard so the problem can't come back. For damage: only the damage pipeline may change health, enforced by the compiler, plus one test that god mode survives every source of harm.
