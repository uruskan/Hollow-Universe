## Consolidated lessons with concrete checks

1. **Whole-body grip, not isolated tool rotation.** The gun examples show continuous mechanical support through hand, wrist, forearm and shoulder. For Hollow's tools, evaluate the right-hand socket, wrist angle, elbow route, blade/head orientation, projected scale and whether the handle passes through the actual grip together. First-person and external views require separate checks. The approved metal axe/pickaxe screenshots should remain the local baseline. No axe appears in this assigned trailer segment.
2. **Distinct motion states.** Carry, raise-to-aim, firing/recoil, handling/reload, and movement occupy meaningfully different poses. Low-carried launcher rising at 113.93 is a clear example. Proposed tool analogue: neutral hold, walk, sprint, wind-up, contact, follow-through, recover; one sine bob plus one rotation cannot express all of these convincingly.
3. **Effects are timelines.** Initial flash is very short; emission/tracer travels; impact follows; debris/smoke/fire can persist. Local lighting must share the event position. Visible examples at 108.65, 112.5, 114.55, 123.35, 125.6 and 128.1–129.1 provide distinct stages. Exact audio sync remains unverified by this visual study.
4. **Terrain works at three scales.** Large stepped rocky ridges and valleys; medium irregular grass/tree masses and bare routes; small stones, flowers and ground variation. The 107.5 frame is especially useful. Avoid a homogeneous carpet of equal-size flora and equally spaced trunks.
5. **Weather/biome material distribution matters.** Snow shots show patchy cover around cracked concrete and worn/bare traffic areas. The warm grassland, winter industrial, desert dusk, and blue-night scenes differ in material context and light, while spatial rules remain coherent.
6. **Night is a contrast hierarchy.** Cool silhouette/background layer, small persistent practical lights, then short intense warm action lights. Fire changes a room's state. The reference is not justification for making all nights brighter or copying cinematic exposure settings.
7. **Construction shows function and age.** Stone load-bearing mass, patchwork corrugated metal, braces, pipes, rivets, windows, wear at seams and streaks below edges. Materials should agree with geometry and scale. Damage changes openings and cover, not only texture and health bars.
8. **Cinematic polish is not a gameplay setting mandate.** Depth of field, low-camera shots, cuts and motion blur often heighten the trailer. Preserve the user's ground UI requirement and aiming clarity; the trailer's missing HUD is not evidence to remove it.
