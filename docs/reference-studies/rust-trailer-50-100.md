## Cross-sequence observations

### Held motion and actions — seen

- Multiple explicit transitions are shown: bow/arrow handling and raise; bow lowering during movement; crossbow carry-to-aim; large gun raise-to-aim; magazine handling; social gesture; firearm return to carry. The beginning and ending of actions are visible, not just their peak.
- Support hand has an actual job during reload (66.750–67.833); primary hand keeps grip while secondary hand leaves and returns. Thumb/finger orientation matters in bow handling and thumbs-up (58.1; 68.4–69.2).
- Carry leaves central traversal information open; aiming intentionally centers and enlarges the weapon/sight. Fast action can temporarily fill the frame, but it returns to a stable readable pose.
- FPS action response is composed with camera motion: moving around cover, entering/exiting doorways, recoil, hit response and target tracking. Third-person bodies visibly share load through torso/shoulders, with locomotion and aim happening together.
- Running team (70.517–73.300) is a useful **third-person** locomotion reference. It does not establish detailed first-person axe walk/run motion; no axe chopping occurs in this assigned 50-second section.
- Gun effects have separate phases: a brief bright flash, a return to ordinary lighting, ejected moving casing, then lingering/thinning smoke where relevant. These components do not all persist for the same duration.

### Environment/material composition — seen

- Depth is repeatedly established with distinct foreground occluder, middle subject, distant landmark: timber door vs ambusher; tarp crate vs fighter vs rocky hill; boat vs freighter; palm/wires vs plane.
- Dry terrain has broad low relief and a few identifiable rock shapes, with small scrub arranged in clumps and exposed ground between. It is not uniformly carpeted with grass or rocks.
- Woodland glimpse through braces has varied tree trunk color/size, mid-height foliage and ferns, ground stones, mottled shade and paths of exposed soil. Vegetation creates layers, not one flat wall.
- Industrial spaces use roof gaps, truss rhythms, crates at varied heights and ground weeds to generate understandable sightlines. Dilapidation is expressed by missing panels, stains and wear, beyond simply tinting material brown.
- Different material roles remain legible: fabric tarp folds; wood grain/braces/plank edges; metallic exposed edges and barrels; diffuse rubble and grass; glossy/reflective water. Warm and cool illumination change their appearance without erasing identity.
- Night is substantially darker outside local light reach. Torch and flashlight make practical pools/cones of information. Underwater light and haze create a similarly bounded exploration space.
- Sunset exterior combines warm rim/direct lighting and cool distant atmosphere. Day exterior is more neutral, with bright sky and readable shadows. References show deliberate differences by time/place rather than a single look applied everywhere.

### Systems/gamefeel — seen versus inferred

Seen: players use cover, doors/gates expose or occlude routes, social gestures are readable, airdrop descent attracts moving players, material upgrades have localized dust feedback, turret direction/state is visible, boat wake explains motion and scale.

Inference/proposal: these suggest player-readable cause/effect and landmarks are as important to atmosphere as asset resolution. They do **not** prove exact combat balance, hitboxes, AI rules, crafting costs, resource economy, sound mix, network quality, terrain generation algorithm or whether every shot is unedited gameplay. Several black/fade/cut transitions clearly belong to trailer editing.

## Proposed Hollow Universe lessons (not newly confirmed requirements)

1. Preserve accepted metal-tool rest silhouettes. Judge new tool profiles across the entire transition loop: equip → carry → walk/start/stop → run → swing → hit/miss → recovery. Match hand behavior to the task and avoid solving only an isolated screenshot.
2. Use shared presentation layers for gait, look lag and settling, applied to the complete arms/item assembly when preserving source skeletal motion. Keep the accepted grip and strike authority unchanged.
3. Make the active hand and support hand responsibilities explicit. The reference reload/gesture/bow sequences motivate authored independent hand motion rather than permanent fists or rigidly attached support palms.
4. Adopt a short list of environment composition tests: foreground/midground/background separation; open paths through clustered vegetation; material-specific highlights; local pools of night light; recognizable large landforms.
5. Build combat/gather feedback as separable stages: anticipation/movement, actual accepted contact, short flash/impact, moving debris/casing, longer smoke/dust/recovery. This is a visual lesson; exact sound timing still needs the video's audio.
6. Favor visible physical changes for world actions: rotating gates, different upgrade materials, localized construction dust, turret rotation/status and vessel wakes. Keep necessary UI preserved rather than replacing it with trailer-style minimal display.
7. For playtest evidence, include motion sequences at full speed plus frame contact sheets. Check frame coverage and readability while looking/turning, not only frozen front-facing poses.

## Evidence limits and follow-ups

- All frames in the assigned span are accounted for via adjacent labelled thumbnails. Seven named full-resolution frames resolved ambiguity in weapon shape, hand handling, night location and gate/material details.
- Sound was not available in these sheets; separate audiovisual playback is required for chop, footsteps, swish/impact timing, acoustic space and mix.
- Terrain/gait facts in these notes are visible facts at trailer scale. Exact degrees/metres/timing curves, tool handles/finger contacts, normal maps and networking require other evidence.
